Nevertheless, Neverthemore

I don't know when it started, but I noticed that it's been raining for a few days now. I don't go outside much, so I suppose it makes sense. But seriously... it hasn't stopped.

I looked out the window today and people were going on about their day like this was nothing out of the usual. 

Well, okay, they probably got used to it by now. But some people are walking without umbrellas nor raincoats, like, half of them?

Whatever, it's none of my business, anyway.

It's still raining, of course, I still hear the tiny wet splash of droplets against the leaves of the trees.

I've grown a habit of pulling up my chair to the window and looking at the little red dot in the sky. 

It's faint, but it's, like, positioned exactly at the top of the hills, so I guess it's a station of some sort that they built recently.

I have tea sometimes, maybe some biscuits if I'm hungry, but usually it's a small break from my seemingly endless work.

I ran out of eggs, so I had to go to Roger's today. It's a shame, I was nearly done with my manuscript, but the hunger was killing me.

It's such a small store, Roger's, but you'd be surprised at the selection of items you can find there, imported stuff too. 

Mr. Roger was there, coincidentally, ringing up a customer.

"What's with the umbrella?" He laughed,  pointing at my old worn out umbrella. I looked at it and laughed too.

"Senior to my father, even," I replied. "It's seen decades of rain, but it's still kicking. But I should probably buy a backup while I'm here, in case it gives up."

Then, I had a sudden realization, my mouth dropping, but I burst out into a cackle soon after.

"Ooooh, Mr. Roger! you have a way of getting people to buy things!"

Mr. Roger looked a bit puzzled at first, glancing outside for the briefest of moments, before turning to me with a strained smile and a nod.

It was a bit weird, but he's getting old, so I just chalked it up to him doing old man things.

Ms. June visited today.

Apparently she was pretty rattled by the confrontation with Mrs. Nuo.

“She's always been such a sweet lady... I treated her like she was my own mother...” She said, “I saw you peeking out of the stairwell that day, and you looked just as baffled, so I just- wanted to talk to you... to not feel crazy.”

“Ah, I see,” I replied, nodding with sympathy. “A lot of people have been acting weird lately. Like, the other day when the clouds were the colours of the rainbow-"

“It was black.”

“Huh...?” I sorta froze with shock.

“It was black.” Ms. June repeated. “Isn't that weird? I mean, it's always dark with all the rain, but it was as if a black hole emerged that day.”

“No, it was a rainbow-" I desperately fished in my closet for the camera, and shoved it into her hands with the picture on display. “See? It wasn't black.”

Ms. June looked at the picture for a while. I couldn't read her expression, but I could tell she was thinking a lot. She finally looked at me again.

“I've been feeling scared recently.” She spoke in a solemn voice all of a sudden. “Even if we have differences, I- I wanted to know if it would be okay if I visited you regularly- I'll just stay here for most of the day and I won't bother you. Is that okay?”

I was silent. I didn't know what to say.

“No, I'm sorry, you would disturb my work.”

Ms. June never visited me again.
